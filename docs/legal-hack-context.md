## 1. What we're building

**One line:** An onchain claim verification and evidence service. It turns public crypto claims into findings that hold up in front of lawyers, regulators and courts.

**Input:** a public claim, for example "$10M volume", "2,700 new users", "no wash trading" or "decentralized liquidity".
**Output:** a sealed integrity report. Every claim gets a verdict, a confidence level, an explicit **Proven / Not proven** split, and a jurisdiction-specific evidence certificate.

**Core pitch:** *Anyone can make a chart. We produce findings that survive cross-examination.*

**Context**
- Built for Legal Hack 2026 (DoraHacks). Primary track: **LegalTech & RegTech**. Secondary track: **AI x Blockchain**.
- Judges and sponsors include blockchain law firms. Prizes include investor pitching.
- **Presented by our lawyer co-founder** as a problem he observed in practice. The engineers are the technical co-founders.
- Runs on Sandworm Labs' data infrastructure through the Sandworm MCP. It is a **separate product, not Sandworm**. On the page, Sandworm appears only as "Data infrastructure by Sandworm Labs" in the footer or a small trust line. No Sandworm branding or colours anywhere else.

## 2. How it works (the method)

This is the product's differentiator, and the landing page has to communicate it.

1. **Claim intake.** Extract checkable claims from a pitch deck, press release, whitepaper or investor update. The legal layer decides which claim types are material: user counts, volume, decentralization, reserves.
2. **Pre-register the method onchain.** Hash the claim, the tests and the thresholds, then commit that hash to a smart contract *before any data is touched*. This proves the method wasn't tuned after seeing results, so nobody can say "you went looking for fraud."
3. **Investigate.** An AI agent runs a reusable battery of tests through the Sandworm MCP:
   - **Cohort vs baseline behaviour:** one-and-done wallets, identical amounts, machine timing, retention.
   - **Funding-trail convergence:** first funder, up to 4 hops back, with exchanges and hubs excluded.
   - **Circularity:** does money loop back to the people who placed the orders?
   - **Concentration:** dependence on one counterparty.
4. **Seal and reveal.** Publish the report hash and verdict against the earlier commitment. The result is a tamper-evident, timestamped record that the method came before the result.
5. **Legal layer.**
   - Maps each verdict to a legal category: material misstatement, disclosure gap, or concentration risk.
   - Generates an evidence certificate for the jurisdiction.
   - Enforces house language: "indicates" / "consistent with", **never "proves fraud"**. Findings must stay defensible and non-defamatory.

**Principles that must show on the page**
- Every finding states what is **proven** and what is **not proven**.
- Thresholds are fixed before running, and that is shown publicly.
- Reports correct themselves when a later test overturns an earlier reading. That's a feature, not an embarrassment.

## 3. Jurisdictions

The product is global; the legal layer is a set of swappable jurisdiction modules.

- **Nigeria (lead, deepest).**
  - Evidence Act 2011, **Section 84**: certificate for computer-generated evidence.
  - ISA 2025: digital assets treated as securities, so disclosure accuracy matters.
  - Nigeria has heavy stablecoin flows.
- **United States (second module, proves portability).** Federal Rules of Evidence **902(13)** (certified records generated by an electronic process) and **902(14)** (data verified by hash). Presented as "built to the rules, pending US counsel review", not as the lawyer's own expertise.
- Page line: **"Built in Nigeria, where the problem is acute. Designed so the legal layer swaps per jurisdiction."**

## 4. Who it's for

- Due-diligence counsel (VC, M&A) verifying a target's onchain claims.
- Regulators reviewing digital-asset disclosures.
- Litigators in misrepresentation or fraud disputes who need admissible onchain evidence.
- Exchanges and listing teams vetting projects.

**Market gap:** Chainalysis, TRM, Elliptic, Crystal and Arkham sell to compliance teams and law enforcement: screening, monitoring, tracing stolen funds. Nobody sells *claim verification with defensible, admissible findings* to the lawyer.

## 5. Demo case study (anonymized — do NOT name the real protocol)

Real investigation, anonymized as **"Protocol P"** (a stablecoin off-ramp) and **"App A"** (a retail app integrated with it). Use these figures in the hero, the case-study section and the verification log.

- **Claim:** App A gained ~2,700 new users in two months.
- **Finding:**
  - 79% of the spike wallets were single-purpose, against 11% of the baseline (7×).
  - One funder seeded 90% of App A's users and almost no users of any other app on the protocol.
  - 99% of the spike wallets never signed a transaction themselves.
- **Verdict:** the wallets are operator-managed, not independent users, so the claim overstates the user base.
- **Proven:** the wallets are not independent self-custody users onchain.
- **Not proven:** whether real customers stand behind them (custodial design vs. created wallets). The answer lives off-chain: KYC records, bank payouts.
- **Self-correction moment:** an earlier test attributed the spike to an outside platform. A later pre-registered test overturned that, and the report says so.
- **Second finding:** headline volume overstated by **23%**, because refunded orders were re-sent and counted twice. Settled volume is the honest number.
- **Concentration:** 88% of one asset's fills depend on a single counterparty.
- **No wash trading found.** Showing a clean result builds credibility.

## 6. Design direction

**Light mode.** Paper and documents, not crypto glow. The look should say "evidence", not "hype".

**Two inspiration sources, both supplied as existing code (and Figma for Quantum):**
- **Quantum template (dark AI SaaS):** use the **layout skeleton and its existing animations**.
- **Quixt template (orange dev studio):** use the **texture**:
  - blueprint / thin-stroke isometric line illustrations;
  - corner crop marks on media frames;
  - the numbered process timeline;
  - a terminal block;
  - a giant wordmark footer.
- **Drop from Quixt:** the all-monospace font and the burnt orange.

**Typography**
- Headlines: a sharp serif (Instrument Serif, Newsreader or Source Serif).
- Body: a neutral sans.
- Monospace **only** for evidence data: tx hashes, addresses, commit IDs. Mono means "this is data".

**Colour**
- Background: paper off-white. Text and structure: deep ink navy.
- One sparing accent: seal red / oxblood, used **only** for verdicts and the "Sealed" stamp, so the colour itself signals a finding.
- No glows, starfields or gradient borders. Use hairline borders, soft shadows and whitespace.
- Never grey-on-grey cards: either a white page with off-white cards, or the reverse.

## 9. Build rules for Claude Code

- **Edit the existing template code. Do not regenerate pages from scratch.** Keep the existing animations and wire new content into them.
- Section content lives in **JSON config** (one file per section, or one content file), so copy and data swap without touching components.
- Code style:
  - Labeled banner-comment sections in every file: `// =====================================` / `// ⬢  Section Name` / `// =====================================`.
  - One explanatory comment at the top of a complex block, not scattered inline comments.
  - Hooks go in `hooks/`, never `lib/`. `lib/` is pure logic only.
  - One central types file. New types go there, not in per-feature `types.ts` files.
  - Short functions, DRY, clear separation of concerns.
- No auth, database or payments. This is a marketing page plus a demo.
